Alfa Laval P26 and APV H17 Freshwater Generators: Introduction and Parts Replacement Guide

📅 Oct 28, 2026 ⏱ 12 min min read

The freshwater generator is a key auxiliary machine on ocean-going vessels. It uses waste heat from the main engine jacket cooling water to distill seawater into freshwater suitable for boilers and domestic use, eliminating the cost of bunkering water. The mainstream type today is the plate-type vacuum distillation unit, and the most widely installed models are the Alfa Laval JWP series (with the P26 plate pack) and freshwater generators built with the APV H17 plate pack. This article explains both machines and focuses on what marine engineers need to watch when replacing spare parts.

1. How a Freshwater Generator Works

Both machines share the same principle: vacuum distillation.

  1. Seawater flows through the evaporator plate pack, heated by jacket cooling water (55~95°C). Because the shell holds 85%95% vacuum, seawater boils at only 4060°C
  2. The vapor rises through the demister, which strips out entrained seawater droplets
  3. Clean vapor enters the condenser plate pack and condenses into freshwater, cooled by seawater
  4. The freshwater pump extracts the distillate; the salinometer continuously monitors quality and dumps off-spec water automatically
  5. Brine (concentrated seawater) and air are extracted by the combined brine/air ejector, maintaining the vacuum

Understanding this principle makes most maintenance rules self-evident — any gasket leak, perforated plate, or worn ejector eventually shows up as falling vacuum or high distillate salinity.

2. Alfa Laval P26 Freshwater Generator (JWP-26 Series)

The Alfa Laval JWP series is the industry standard for single-stage vacuum distillation units. The JWP-16 produces 0.510 m3/24h, while the **JWP-26 produces 435 m3/24h** (up to 100 m3/24h on request), the size most commonly found on commercial cargo ships.

Construction

A complete JWP-26 comprises: the shell (combined evaporating and condensing chamber), two titanium plate packs, a two-stage combined brine/air ejector, a freshwater pump with mechanical seal, salinometer control unit, demister, and a chemical dosing connection (anti-scale dosing required when heating water exceeds 75°C).

Capacity can be adjusted by adding or removing plates — a key JWP advantage: shipowners can scale up output without replacing the whole unit.

3. APV H17 Freshwater Generator

The APV H17 plate pack is another common specification for marine freshwater generators, widely installed in APV/SPX shell-type units and FWGs fitted by Chinese shipyards.

H17 Plate Specifications

Item Specification
Plate model APV H17
Corrugation Herringbone, approx. 3 mm depth
Port diameter Approx. φ74 mm
Plate material Titanium Grade 1 (0.5 mm) standard; 316L also available
Gasket material NBR (seawater-resistant) standard
Mounting Hanging design with four-corner interlocking

APV plates feature four-corner interlocking plus peripheral gasket interlocking, with a dovetail hanging area on the upper guide — making misalignment during plate replacement less likely than on many other brands. Still, plates should be installed in numbered sequence.

Whether Alfa Laval or APV H17, the wear parts of a marine freshwater generator are largely the same:

5. Parts Replacement Considerations (Key Section)

1. Plates: Test Before You Decide

Plates are the most expensive and critical FWG parts. Titanium itself resists seawater corrosion extremely well; actual failures are mostly pinholes and stress cracks:

  • After removal, perform dye penetrant testing (DPT) or pressure testing on every plate — micro-porosity is invisible to the eye but enough to let seawater contaminate the distillate
  • If a single plate is perforated, an emergency fix is to remove that plate (remove its neighbor on both sides to keep flow symmetric), with the total removed not exceeding 10% of the pack; replace the plates at the next opportunity
  • Never clean with hydrofluoric acid or strong alkali — titanium is attacked by HF; citric-acid or oxalic-acid cleaners below 50°C are the safe choice
  • Do not scrub titanium plates hard with wire brushes; scratches accelerate crevice corrosion

2. Gaskets: Right Material, Even Fit

  • NBR nitrile rubber is the standard for FWG gaskets (seawater-resistant, -15~120°C); do not casually substitute EPDM, and never mix the two
  • Replace at 2~3 years, or earlier if gaskets show hardening, cracking, or deep compression set — do not wait for a leak
  • For glued gaskets, apply a thin, even layer of adhesive, fit at half-tack, and allow to cure before reassembly
  • Tighten the compression plate diagonally and in stages to the specified final dimension; over-tightening damages plates and gaskets

3. FW Pump Mechanical Seal: Small Part, Big Impact

Mechanical seal leakage is among the most common FWG failures. Keep sealing faces clean, protect the O-rings during fitting, and check free rotation afterwards. Keep one spare set on board — an emergency swap takes only about half an hour.

4. Demister: Fouling Directly Degrades Water Quality

A fouled demister lets entrained droplets through, raising distillate salinity. Remove and rinse with fresh water at low pressure every 1~2 years; replace if deformed or damaged. Watch the orientation and edge sealing during refit to prevent vapor short-circuiting.

5. Salinometer Electrode and Zinc Anode: Do Not Overlook These Small Items

  • The salinometer electrode (e.g. Alfa Laval SL20) reads falsely when scaled; clean regularly with a soft cloth and fresh water and verify against a standard resistor per the manual
  • The zinc anode inside the shell is the last line of defense against galvanic corrosion — replace it when half-consumed or loose. It is cheap; forgetting it is not

6. Ejector: Check It First When Vacuum Drops

When vacuum cannot be reached, the ejector is the usual suspect — worn nozzle, corroded diffuser, or insufficient motive-water pressure. Replace the nozzle when its bore wears more than about 5% oversize, and verify motive-water pressure against the nameplate.

7. General Rules

  • Quote parts precisely: for Alfa Laval, provide the machine unit number; for APV, provide plate model + quantity + clamping length. Photos of the plate corrugation help
  • Replace complete sets, not mixed old and new: mixed gaskets cause uneven compression and recurring leaks
  • Always test after reassembly: pull vacuum above 85% and hold for 30 minutes, then run a production trial and confirm distillate salinity before returning the unit to service
  • Keep records: log vacuum, evaporating temperature, output, and salinity after each overhaul — trend comparison catches problems early

6. Conclusion

The Alfa Laval JWP-26 (P26) and APV H17 share the same principle and similar wear parts. The keys to parts management:

  1. Do not run on damaged plates — DPT test, replace or symmetrically remove perforated plates
  2. Replace gaskets on schedule with the right material — NBR, tightened to the specified dimension
  3. Do not ignore the small parts — salinometer electrode, zinc anode, mechanical seal, ejector nozzle: cheap but critical
  4. Verify with tests after replacement — vacuum test plus salinity check means the job is done
