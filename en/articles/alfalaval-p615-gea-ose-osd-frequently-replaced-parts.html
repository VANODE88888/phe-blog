Alfa Laval P615 and GEA OSE/OSD Separators: Frequently Replaced Parts with Part Numbers

📅 Nov 3, 2026 ⏱ 12 min min read

The separator is one of the easiest machines to bring down with a tiny part: a failed solenoid valve, a worn belt, an aged O-ring, or fouled bowl discs will each trip it offline. This article lists the frequently replaced parts and their part numbers for the two mainstream marine separators — the Alfa Laval P615 and GEA Westfalia OSE/OSD (Alfa Laval data taken from the official P615 spare parts catalogue SVIB; GEA data from actual purchase records) — so engineers can order directly against part numbers.

1. Alfa Laval P615: Frequently Replaced Parts

The P615 belongs to the Alfa Laval P series (P605/P615/P617) with EPC 60 control. Intermediate Service is due every 2,000 hours or 3 months; Major Service every 8,000 hours or 12 months.

6. Solenoid and Flow Valves

The operating-water solenoid valves, flow regulating valve, and EPC 60 controller belong to the module system (the Flex module SRK / flow regulating system), not the separator SVIB catalogue. When ordering, quote the module machine unit number + EPC 60 controller serial number so the system catalogue can identify the part — quoting only the separator model is not enough. Many ships simply order against the part number stamped on the valve nameplate.

3. Replacement Considerations

1. Belts (Both Brands)

  • Never lever a belt on with a screwdriver — release the tightener first, fit the belt, then restore tension. A belt with damaged cords is a waiting failure
  • While changing the belt, inspect the pulleys and tightener bearings — a new belt on worn pulleys lasts half as long
  • Alfa Laval belt numbers differ between 50 Hz (26016922) and 60 Hz (26016923); GEA belt numbers differ by model — always match the motor frequency and machine model

2. Solenoid Valves

  • De-energize and depressurize before removal; tag the wiring
  • Distinguish the "valve assembly" from the "solenoid coil": often only the coil is burned out, and replacing just the coil (e.g. GEA 0018-6654-010) is far cheaper than the whole block
  • After refitting, test manually and confirm crisp opening/closing water response before service. A sluggish response usually means a clogged pilot orifice — clean the internal strainer
  • If solenoid valves fail repeatedly, check the operating water quality first — scaling kills valves; flush regularly or treat the water

3. Flow Valve / Operating Water System

  • A sticking flow regulating valve causes insufficient opening water and incomplete sludge shots; check the valve internals and strainer regularly
  • Operating water pressure must stay within the nameplate range (2~8 bar for the P615); when pressure is unstable, check the pressure regulator before blaming the solenoid valve

4. Bowl Discs

  • Clean each disc individually with a soft scraper — never wire brushes; the discs are only 0.5 mm thick and scratches shorten their life drastically
  • Refit in the original order with the correct corrugation orientation, and verify the lock ring reaches its marked position
  • Replace deformed or edge-chipped discs immediately — mixing damaged discs ruins separation efficiency

5. O-rings and Seals

  • Replace all O-rings as a complete set at every IS service; refitting used O-rings is gambling on the next leak
  • Lubricate new O-rings with silicone grease in the groove to prevent twisting and scratching during assembly
  • GEA polyamide gaskets (0007-1945-750) require the dedicated fitting tool — do not hammer or force them

6. Friction Elements / Clutch Shoes

  • Alfa Laval friction elements (54406001): 4 pieces for 50 Hz, 2 pieces for 60 Hz — the quantity differs
  • Never touch friction faces with oily hands; oil-contaminated friction elements mean slipping, overheating, then breakage
  • Replace GEA clutch shoes (0021-3293-870) as soon as wear approaches the rivets

4. Ordering Tips

  1. Alfa Laval: quote the machine unit number for the separator body; for module parts (solenoid/flow valves) quote the module number + controller serial number
  2. GEA: quote the full model + serial number (e.g. OSD 18-91-067 / Ser.No.9051-116); part numbers can differ between design years of the same model
  3. Stock onboard: 2 belts, 1 solenoid coil, 1 full O-ring set, 1 friction element set, 1 lock ring — these five are the hardest to source in an emergency
