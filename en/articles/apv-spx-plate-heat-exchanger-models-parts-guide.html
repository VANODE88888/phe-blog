APV and SPX Plate Heat Exchangers and Parts: Common Models and Selection Guide

📅 Nov 12, 2026 ⏱ 13 min min read

APV is one of the oldest names in plate heat exchangers, with a very large installed base in food, dairy, brewing and marine applications. Owners and contractors often ask one question when ordering spares: the nameplate says APV on one unit and SPX on another — are the parts the same? This article explains the brand relationship, the model system, the common models (marked with ★), and the plate and gasket parts.

1. How APV and SPX Relate

Date Event
History APV, a long-established Danish heat transfer and fluid equipment maker
2008 APV joined the SPX group
2015 SPX FLOW spun off from SPX and listed independently
Today APV continues as an SPX FLOW brand

Conclusion: what the market calls "APV plate heat exchangers" and "SPX plate heat exchangers" is the same product line. An older nameplate reading APV and a newer one reading SPX FLOW APV with the same model number are the same plate type, and plates and gaskets are fully interchangeable.

SPX FLOW also owns Waukesha Cherry-Burrell (dairy/hygienic equipment), Johnson Pump and Lightnin mixers, but the plate heat exchanger line is APV.

2. Reading an APV Model Code

APV models are generally "letters + numbers + suffix":

  • Letters indicate the plate family (A/B/H/N/Q/M/J/SR/R/P/TR/T)
  • Numbers roughly correspond to single-plate area ×100 (H17 ≈ 0.17 m2, N50 ≈ 0.50 m2)
  • Suffixes denote versions: L/S = long/short plate, D/E = deep/shallow groove (Q030D deep, Q030E shallow), G usually indicates glued gaskets (SR6GL), plus special A/V/N/P versions

⚠️ A different suffix means a different plate type, and gaskets are generally not interchangeable. Always quote the full model — Q080E cannot be shortened to Q080.

5. Plate and Gasket Parts

Plate materials

Material Suitable media
304 SS Domestic hot water, general industrial water
316L SS Cooling water, process water (most common)
Titanium Seawater, brine, freshwater generators
SMO 254 High chloride, dilute sulphuric acid
Hastelloy C-276 Aggressive media

Standard thickness is 0.5 mm; some large plates come in 0.6/0.7 mm.

Gasket materials

Material Temp Application
NBR -15~120°C Water, seawater, oil, food (FDA/3A available)
EPDM -25~170°C Hot water, steam, acids/alkalis
HNBR -15~140°C Hot oil, hot water
FKM (Viton) -5~180°C Aggressive media, solvents

For food and dairy, always specify FDA or 3A certified food-grade gaskets; industrial gaskets must not be mixed in.

Other parts

  • Frames and tie bolts: bolt stretch determines clamping force; corroded or damaged threads must be replaced
  • Carrier bars: the plate suspension rails; wear causes plate misalignment
  • Port rings / frame liners: the usual location for connection corrosion and leakage
  • Double-wall safety plates: e.g. H12 WDU, N35 Dousafety, for duties where two media must never mix (drinking water, pharmaceutical)

6. Ordering and Replacement Tips

  1. Quote the full suffix: Q080E ≠ Q080D, SR6GL ≠ SR6AG, TR9-I/II/III have different plate lengths
  2. Give the clamping dimension A and plate count: on units that have been re-plated, only A plus plate count identifies the configuration
  3. Match plate material to media: seawater requires titanium — do not substitute 316L
  4. Certifications for food duty: gaskets need FDA/3A documentation; food-grade and industrial grades must not be mixed
  5. Record dimension A before opening, compress back to it after regasketing; tighten diagonally in stages with equal A at the corners
  6. No hydrochloric acid cleaning — use neutral or dedicated cleaners, soft brushes in one direction, and check each plate for pinholes
  7. Never mix old and new gaskets — replace as a complete set

7. Summary

  • APV and SPX are one company: APV joined SPX in 2008 and became part of SPX FLOW in 2015; same model means interchangeable parts
  • Only a handful matter day to day: H12/H17 (hygienic, freshwater generators), N25/N35/N50 (general), Q030/Q055/Q080 (HVAC), A085/A145 and J107/J185 (large), R5/R8/R10 (high pressure), SR6/SR9 (food), TR9 (marine)
  • Suffixes decide fit: misreading D/E, L/S or GL/AG means the part will not fit
