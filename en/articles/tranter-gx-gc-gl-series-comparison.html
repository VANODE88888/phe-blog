Tranter GX vs GC vs GL Series Plate Heat Exchangers: Differences, Technical Parameters & Selection Guide

📅 Aug 5, 2026 ⏱ 18 min read

Tranter is a globally leading plate heat exchanger manufacturer with a product line spanning gasketed, brazed, and welded types. Among these, the GX Series, GC Series, and GL Series are the three most commonly discussed product lines, yet many engineers find them confusing during selection. This article provides a systematic comparison across four dimensions: structural principles, technical parameters, application scenarios, and maintenance costs.

In short: GX is the "all-rounder," GC is the "compact high-efficiency" option, and GL is the "extreme conditions specialist."

2. GX Series — Gasketed Plate Heat Exchangers

2.1 Structural Features

The GX Series is Tranter's classic gasketed plate heat exchanger. Plates are sealed with elastomer gaskets and clamped together in a frame using tie bolts. Its core advantage is removable maintenance — when plates foul or become damaged, simply loosen the bolts to clean or replace individual plates.

2.2 Plate Design

GX Series plates feature Tranter's patented chevron corrugation design. The alternating herringbone pattern creates strong turbulence in the fluid between plates, achieving heat transfer coefficients of 5,000-7,000 W/(m²·K), far exceeding shell-and-tube heat exchangers.

2.4 Gasket Material Selection

The GX Series offers multiple gasket materials for different media:

  • NBR (Nitrile Butadiene Rubber): General-purpose, suitable for water and oil media, working temperature ≤110°C
  • EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer): Resistant to hot water and steam, ≤150°C, not oil-resistant
  • HNBR (Hydrogenated Nitrile): High-temperature oil resistant, ≤150°C
  • Viton (Fluorocarbon): Resistant to highly corrosive media, ≤180°C

2.5 Advantages and Limitations

Advantages:

  • Removable for cleaning, low maintenance cost
  • Individual plate and gasket replacement, high cost-effectiveness
  • Flexible heat transfer area adjustment by adding/removing plates
  • Wide range of plate material options

Limitations:

  • Gaskets have aging cycles (typically 3-5 years replacement)
  • Maximum working pressure limited by gasket seal (≤25 bar)
  • Not ideal for highly corrosive media in frequent duty cycles

3. GC Series — Brazed Plate Heat Exchangers

3.1 Structural Features

The GC Series is Tranter's brazed plate heat exchanger product line. Unlike the GX Series, GC plates are fused together in a vacuum furnace using copper or nickel brazing material at high temperature, completely eliminating gaskets. The seal between plates is achieved by brazed joints, enabling higher working pressures.

3.2 Brazing Process

The GC Series offers two brazing materials:

  • Copper Brazing (Cu): Standard type, suitable for water, glycol solutions, and conventional media, cost-effective
  • Nickel Brazing (Ni): Corrosion-resistant type, suitable for ammonia, strong alkalis, and corrosive media, slightly higher cost

3.4 Advantages and Limitations

Advantages:

  • Compact structure, only 1/3 the volume of equivalent GX Series
  • No gaskets, higher pressure resistance (up to 30 bar)
  • Brazed joints provide excellent sealing for refrigerants
  • Lower cost than equivalent GX Series (no frame or bolts needed)

Limitations:

  • Not removable for cleaning; internal fouling requires chemical cleaning or total replacement
  • Not suitable for media with particulates or scaling tendencies
  • Limited plate material options (mainly 316L and nickel)

4. GL Series — Welded Plate Heat Exchangers

4.1 Structural Features

The GL Series is Tranter's welded plate heat exchanger. Plates are sealed using laser welding technology with continuous weld beads along plate edges. Unlike brazing, GL Series uses welding material of the same alloy as the plates, producing weld strength equal to the base plate material, capable of withstanding extreme high-temperature and high-pressure conditions.

4.2 Welding Process

The GL Series uses laser beam welding technology:

  • Weld penetration depth reaches 1.5 times the plate thickness
  • Weld width is only 0.5-1.0mm, minimally affecting heat transfer area
  • Small heat-affected zone, preserving plate corrosion resistance
  • Capable of welding stainless steel, titanium alloys, and Inconel

4.4 Advantages and Limitations

Advantages:

  • No gaskets, completely eliminating leakage risk
  • Withstands high temperatures and pressures (up to 40 bar / 300°C)
  • Weld material matches plate material, no galvanic corrosion
  • Suitable for highly corrosive media (with titanium or Inconel plates)

Limitations:

  • Higher cost (approximately 2-3 times the GX Series)
  • Not removable for cleaning; requires chemical cleaning or high-pressure water flushing
  • Longer manufacturing lead time (primarily custom production)

5.3 Selection Decision Flow

Step 1: Confirm Media Type

  • Clean water/glycol → All three suitable
  • Particulate/scaling media → Prefer GX (removable cleaning)
  • Refrigerants (Freon, ammonia) → GC (copper/nickel brazed)
  • Thermal oil/high-temperature media → GL (welded, high-temp resistant)

Step 2: Confirm Operating Temperature and Pressure

  • Temperature <180°C, pressure <25 bar → GX
  • Temperature <220°C, pressure <30 bar → GC
  • Temperature <300°C, pressure <40 bar → GL

Step 3: Confirm Maintenance Requirements

  • Needs regular cleaning and plate replacement → GX
  • Maintenance-free, long-term operation after installation → GC or GL
  • Scaling media but requires welded structure → GL (with chemical cleaning)

6. Typical Application Cases

6.1 Marine Central Cooler — GX Series

In marine central cooling systems, the seawater side is highly susceptible to scaling and marine organism growth. The GX Series removable structure allows crew members to periodically open the unit for plate cleaning and EPDM gasket replacement. Typically, 316L stainless steel or titanium plates with EPDM gaskets are selected, with a design life exceeding 20 years.

6.2 Commercial Refrigeration Condenser — GC Series

In commercial air conditioning and chiller units, the refrigerant side requires high sealing integrity to prevent leakage. The GC Series copper-brazed structure perfectly accommodates refrigerants such as R134a and R410A, while its compact design significantly reduces unit size. Typical selections are GC50 or GC51, with 3-5 m² heat transfer area meeting 50-100kW cooling capacity requirements.

6.3 Petrochemical Thermal Oil Heat Exchange — GL Series

Thermal oil systems in petrochemical plants operate at 250-300°C, far exceeding the temperature limit of GX gaskets (180°C). The GL Series welded structure has no gasket limitations and, combined with 316L or Inconel plates, can operate stably long-term. The GL230 large model meets heat transfer area requirements of 500 m² or more.

GC Series Maintenance Recommendations

  • Regularly check inlet/outlet pressure differential; increased differential indicates possible internal blockage
  • Use mild acid or alkaline solution for circulating chemical cleaning
  • Avoid solid particles in media (filter precision recommended ≤100μm)
  • Replace entire unit if external leakage is detected (non-repairable)

GL Series Maintenance Recommendations

  • Periodically inspect weld bead appearance for cracks
  • Perform hydrostatic testing after chemical cleaning to confirm weld integrity
  • Control temperature change rate to ≤50°C/h to avoid thermal stress damage
  • Use online monitoring systems to track inlet/outlet temperature differential changes

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Can the GX Series be converted to brazed type?

No. GX Series plate corrugation design and plate thickness are optimized for gasket sealing. Brazing requires a dedicated plate design. If a brazed product is needed, please select the GC Series.

Q2: Can the GC Series be used for seawater cooling?

Copper-brazed GC Series is not recommended for seawater (copper corrodes in seawater). If brazed construction is required, choose nickel-brazed models, or select the GX Series with titanium plates.

Q3: Are GL and GX Series plates interchangeable?

No. GL Series welding plates feature edge weld groove structures, while GX Series plate edges are designed for gasket sealing. The two have different plate geometries.

Q4: How to decide which series to choose?

The simplest decision method: For water-washable applications choose GX, for refrigerants choose GC, for high-temperature/high-pressure choose GL. If uncertain, please contact us for professional selection calculations.

Conclusion

Tranter's GX, GC, and GL series each have their own positioning — there is no question of "which is better," only "which is more suitable for your operating conditions." Selection requires comprehensive consideration of media type, temperature and pressure, maintenance requirements, and economics. For most conventional water cooling applications, the GX Series is the first choice; the refrigeration industry prefers the GC Series; high-temperature, high-pressure, and highly corrosive conditions require the GL Series.
